# LNURLcash over FIPS

27 September 2026, branch `feat/fips` (stacked on the rebuild). Step 1 of
Felix's plan "FIPS in LNURL, then LNURL in TollGate".

## What FIPS is, as far as it matters here

[FIPS](https://fips.network) (jmcorgan/fips, MIT, v0.5.1 of 6 September
2026) is a self-organizing encrypted mesh over UDP, TCP, Ethernet, WiFi,
Bluetooth, Tor or Nym. A node's identity is a Nostr keypair. The daemon maps
each npub to an address in `fd00::/8` on a TUN adapter (`fips0`) and answers
`<npub>.fips` through its own DNS responder. Traffic is encrypted and
authenticated per hop (Noise IK) and end to end (Noise XK), so a `.fips` name
is the endpoint's key: self-authenticating, like an onion name. Installers
exist for Debian/Ubuntu, RPM, OpenWrt (`ipk`, `apk`), macOS, FreeBSD and
Windows; Android as an embedded crate, iOS not yet.

`fd00::/8` is the general IPv6 unique-local range. An address literal there
says nothing about FIPS; only the `.fips` name does.

## What Bearlett does

`http://<npub>.fips` is admitted like `http://….onion` (`plainHttpHost` in
`src/lnurl/net.ts`): LUD-17 links on a `.fips` host map to http, a Lightning
Address at a FIPS mint resolves to http, and spends bind the `.fips` name
(the mint's key-path signatures and certificates work as on any other host).
A bare `fd00::` address stays refused.

## How it was tried

`tests/mesh/run.sh`, also the CI job `mesh`: two FIPS 0.5.1 nodes in two
network namespaces, joined by a veth pair over UDP, each resolving `.fips`
through its own responder. Node B serves the conformance mock mint on its
mesh address only (`proxy.py` forwards `[fips0 address]:80` to the mock on
loopback). Node A runs Bearlett's wallet core against
`http://<npub of B>.fips`: add the mint, mint 50 sat (49 after the fee), hand
out a bearer link `lnurlw://<npub>.fips/w?k1=…`, receive and rotate it in a
second wallet, pay an invoice (split, then melt; 32 000 msat left, to the
msat), recover from the words. All nine checks passed on the first run in
WSL (Ubuntu 24.04, kernel 6.6).
