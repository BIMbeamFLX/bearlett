# Bearlett UI: composable napplets, assets as a play experience, Pay as a Bitcoin wallet

As of 9 September 2026. Status: design draft and recommendation, not an implementation.
Basis: Bearlett `8b02e62` (branch `fix/bearlett-security-boundaries`),
TCG wallet `d753505` (`feature/g-mint-live`),
Nappelin `1b2f2c8`, napplet/naps `a040914` (20 August 2026),
cashu-ts 4.10.1 as installed. Web research of 9 September 2026. Sources in
section 12.

## Result in brief

- **Every function is its own napplet.** Ten napplets plus one per collection,
  all joined through NAP-INTENT and archetypes, nothing hard-wired. Catalogue in
  section 7.
- **One napplet per collection.** Decided on 10 September 2026. A collection
  napplet holds exactly one edition, from one mint, under one catalogue. Mints are
  never mixed inside a napplet; a second collection is a second napplet. Card and
  Reveal stay collection-agnostic surfaces without secrets. Handover lives in the
  collection napplet, because a reply cannot pick one of several handlers of the
  same archetype. Reasoning in section 6.
- **Assets as a play experience.** The cards are the existing NutFT cards of the
  TCG wallet. They are not new tokens.
- **Pay as an uncluttered Bitcoin wallet.** Overview, Send, Receive, Scanner and
  Activity as their own napplets; Lightning with BOLT11 and BOLT12, plus LNURLcash and
  Cashu. BOLT12 is present in cashu-ts 4.10.1 and not yet wired in Bearlett.
- **Animation stack:** timeline compositions after the HyperFrames contract,
  executed with the pure state function of the Nappelin intro instead of GSAP.
  CSS 3D for cards. Three.js 0.182 only where a body is needed.
- **Hard constraint:** a napplet is one inline file in an iframe with
  `sandbox="allow-scripts"` and a CSP with no network, no external scripts, no fonts.
  One wallet window per storage scope. No transactions across windows.

## 1. Constraints

Verbatim from the client in this session: gaming assets are the path. The
user interface is everything: a play-like experience with animation and 3D.
Separate napplet screens, one for assets, one clean like a Bitcoin wallet for
paying, preferably with Lightning built in, BOLT11 and BOLT12. The Nappelin intro
as the reference for the animation stack. Hyperframe style for napplets, to be researched.
Every function its own napplet. Everything
composable. Do not forget intent and archetype, and document them cleanly. No mistakes.

## 2. Scope decision and its consequences

The start prompt locks V1 to LNURLcash and unbound Cashu sats and forbids
silently taking in other units or P2PK tokens. The NutFT cards are
exactly that: Cashu proofs with `amount = 1`, bound by tag to `collection_id`, `asset_id`,
`catalog_uri` and an `asset_binding`, with the holder's P2BK key and
their own keyset units `600B-E1` and `600B-G`
(`TCG600nap/site/nutft-wallet.js:109`, `:581-595`).

Taking them in is therefore an explicit decision of the client, not a
silent extension. The design assumes:

1. The protocol cores stay separate, as recorded in
   [TCG-WALLET-2026-09-09.md](TCG-WALLET-2026-09-09.md). Each collection napplet
   speaks to the NutFT mint of its own edition. The Pay napplets speak to the
   Bearlett core. Neither reaches into the other's store.
2. Both families use the same access and the same backup path from
   [NAPPELIN-INTEGRATION-2026-09-09.md](NAPPELIN-INTEGRATION-2026-09-09.md).
   Login keys and money keys stay separate.
3. Granola, markets and prices stay V2. No floor price. No marketplace.

Patterns adopted, and where they go:

1. Grouping by issuer-verified collection with a deterministic fallback
   and a name priority chain: edition from the signed catalogue, else mint plus
   keyset. Napplet _Collection_.
2. Two layouts, list by collection and gallery grid, sort new and
   alphabetical, pinning. _Collection_.
3. Three reversible stages: automatically hidden, hidden by the user, trash;
   never delete; copy says that hidden does not mean safe. _Collection_; the
   TCG buckets `unreadable` and `invalid` stay separately visible.
4. Verified badge with an honest caveat, derived from signature and
   hash, never from a UI flag. _Card_, one green element.
5. "Do not interact" plus report with a tip for unexpected tokens.
   _Collection_, _Reveal_.
6. Media ladder with a fixed thumbnail size, renderer per type, 3D in the sandbox.
   _Collection_, _Card_; card images always hash-checked.
7. "Refresh metadata" as a user action with the assurance that the asset
   is safe even without an image. _Card_.
8. No single indexer or host: three Blossom mirrors plus hash check, as
   NIP-B7 describes; Rainbow's "delisted equals missing" is the failure case.
9. Send review shows recipient, fee and effect in plain text; warning on a new
   recipient; format check. _Trade_, _Send_.
10. Multi-select on send. _Collection_ → _Trade_.
11. Receive via personal QR and a readable name. _Receive_, _Trade_.
12. Backup as a phrase plus optional encrypted cloud backup with a strong
    password and the copy "we cannot restore it". Core napplets.
13. Non-transferable assets visibly marked; NIP-58 badges even require
    accept before display. Cashu analogy: P2BK-bound means "bound to you".
    _Card_.
14. Accessibility is missing in every review; labelled controls and
    keyboard paths are required here explicitly.

No wallet among Cashu, Nostr and Blossom was found that displays media-bearing
tokens. Minibits binds ecash to the recipient pubkey. Olas uploads natively to
Blossom. NIP-B7 describes hash checking on download. An "NFT on ecash" was
not attested. The card model of the TCG wallet therefore has no direct precedent.

### 4.1 What is not taken from NFT wallets

- **Public provenance chain.** A bearer token has no public owner.
  Provenance here means: signed catalogue, binding hash, checked image hash, state
  at the mint.
- **Possession by display.** A copied card remains spendable. Accept always means
  a swap at the mint. The collection shows mint state.
- **Prices and markets.** No floor prices. No marketplace. No gas fees.
  Mint fees are shown before handover.
- **Spam-hiding as a main function.** Cards come only from signed catalogues.

## 5. HyperFrames as a style reference

The intended name is almost certainly **HyperFrames** by HeyGen: "Write HTML.
Render video. Built for agents." Open source under Apache-2.0. Repository since
10 March 2026. Version 0.8.33 as of today. Nothing with this name exists in
Nostr, napplets, Cashu or Lightning. The remaining "Hyperframe" hits are
unrelated companies (sources 12.5).

### 5.1 What HyperFrames is

- **Authoring contract:** one self-contained HTML file. Root
  `<div data-composition-id data-width data-height data-start data-duration>`;
  children `class="clip"` with `id`, `data-start` (absolute or relative such as
  `intro + 0.5`), `data-duration`, `data-track-index`. Nested compositions
  via `data-composition-src` and `<template>`; variables via
  `data-composition-variables`.
- **Timeline:** one paused GSAP timeline per composition, registered in sync on
  `window.__timelines[id]`; the runtime _seeks_ points in time. It does not play
  them back. Rules: no `Date.now`, no `Math.random`, no `repeat: -1`, only
  transform tweens, no network at runtime.
- **Frame adapter:** WebGL and Three.js via `seekFrame(frame)`; same frame,
  same state. Arbitrary jumps allowed.
- **Rendering:** DOM in headless Chromium per frame, FFmpeg encodes. Live in the browser
  via Studio and `<hyperframes-player>`. That player loads about 400 KB of runtime from the CDN.
- **House style** (`skills/hyperframes-creative/references/*.md`): lock ground, foreground
  and accent before the first line of code; no gradient text, no neon on
  dark, no pure `#000` or `#fff`, no identical card grids, not everything
  centred. Serif plus sans with extreme weight contrast, headlines from 60 px,
  `tabular-nums`. Inter, Roboto, Poppins forbidden; recommended Oswald, League Gothic,
  Archivo Black, Space Mono, IBM Plex Mono. Fonts embedded as data URIs. Three
  depth layers: breathing background with ghost text and grain, middle ground with
  cards, foreground with hairlines and mono metadata. Two to five ambient
  elements per scene, always slowly in motion. Entries 0.3 to 0.6 s with
  overlapping offset, at least three easings per timeline, shader transitions only
  at scene boundaries, otherwise hard cuts.

every visible value is a function of time. That is the ground rule for every
choreography in this draft.

## 6. Composable: ground rules

1. **One napplet, one function.** Each napplet has its own manifest, its own
   storage scope and exactly one purpose. No tab switcher. No router.
2. **No napplet addresses another directly.** Calls go through NAP-INTENT to
   an archetype. The shell picks the user's default handler. In front of every
   button sits `intent.available(archetype)`.
3. **Secrets only in cores.** Two kinds of napplet hold secrets: _Bearlett Wallet_
   (notes, Cashu proofs, journal) and one _collection_ napplet per edition (its own
   card proofs and P2BK keys). Every other napplet receives versioned, display-only
   messages without proofs, seeds, keys or bearer URLs, after the pattern of the
   existing `NoteDesignMessage`.
4. **One napplet per collection.** Decided on 10 September 2026. A collection
   napplet serves exactly one edition, from one mint, under one catalogue. Mints are
   never mixed inside a napplet. A second collection is a second napplet, and the
   shell stacks them as several handlers of the same archetype.

   Three reasons, in order of weight. The storage scope is the strongest: a napplet
   owns exactly one, and "one writer per scope" is the hardest open problem in this
   wallet. One napplet per collection dissolves it, because editions never contended
   for a writer in the first place. Second, the resource policy becomes truthful:
   each artifact declares the single mint it needs, so the host grants one origin
   instead of the union of all of them. Third, it mirrors the issuer. The NutFT mint
   already runs `600B-E1` and `600B-G` as separate instances with separate databases,
   because their identity triples must not collide; re-mixing them in the wallet
   would undo that on the client side.

   The cost is keys and backup: N scopes mean N backups, and N seeds would mean N
   recovery phrases. The resolution is one phrase with a per-collection derivation,
   so one thing is written down, the sandboxes stay separate, and no seed travels
   between them at runtime. Restore is per collection anyway, because NUT-13 scans
   one keyset at a time.
5. **One writer per storage scope.** NAP-STORAGE has no transactions across
   windows. The core therefore holds state, and surfaces ask it.
   Request and reply are two intents: the surface sends to the core archetype,
   the core replies to the surface archetype.
6. **A reply cannot pick one of several handlers.** NAP-INTENT routes an archetype
   to the user's default handler; `handler: "<dTag>"` exists but should require the
   user to authorise cross-napplet targeting. With N collection napplets sharing one
   archetype, any surface that must answer _a specific_ collection is ambiguous.
   This is why Card and Reveal are shared surfaces, they only ever receive, and why
   handover lives inside the collection napplet rather than in a Trade surface.
7. **Bearer secrets travel only where they must.** `napplet:wallet/receive` and
   `napplet:collection/receive` carry the token to be redeemed, as today. A token
   that leaves a wallet is shown by the napplet that holds it. It is never sent to a
   surface.
8. **Confirmation in the core.** Surfaces place requests. The core shows the review,
   demands unlock and executes. `ok` and `handled` mean delivery, never
   success. A new request never replaces a running review.
9. **Archetypes are proposals.** The registry `napplet/naps` (`a040914`) knows
   `note`, `profile`, `dm`, `feed`, `feed-images`, `feed-videos`, `feed-manager`,
   `composer`, `pet`. Every slug below is new, unregistered, and will be submitted as a proposal
   with Boundary, Distinct-from and Actions once an implementation
   exists (status `draft` until then, approval by the maintainer).

## 7. Napplet catalogue with archetypes and intents

Convention throughout: `napplet:<archetype>/<action>` with no query parameters. Payloads
are objects, checked at the receiver as untrusted. `requires` names the
NAP domains of the manifest.

### 7.1 Cores

**The `wallet` archetype's contract is closed at four conventions**, confirmed on
10 September 2026: `napplet:wallet/open`, `/receive`, `/pay` and `/design`. Those
are what `src/napplet/intents.ts:13-18` accepts and what
[NAPPLETS.md](NAPPLETS.md) publishes. The four BOLT12 and view conventions named
in the table below are **proposals, not part of that contract**, and none of
them may be built without a decision that widens it. There is deliberately no
`napplet:wallet/recovery-v1`, and no other napplet may invent one.

Until that decision, the Pay surfaces have no route to the wallet core for
BOLT12, balances or activity. That is the honest state, and it is a smaller
problem than a second, unversioned wallet contract growing by accident.

### 7.4 Display messages (version 1)

Every message has `kind`, `version: 1` and no secrets. Unknown fields are
dropped. Sizes are bounded. The sender is the runtime-confirmed
`sender`.

```ts
/* One held card, as a collection napplet hands it to the Card surface.
   There is no inventory message: a collection renders its own grid from its
   own store, so no list of holdings ever crosses a napplet boundary. */
type CardAsset = {
  asset_id: string
  name: string
  tier: string
  copies_owned: number // from groupOwned()
  state: 'owned' | 'spent' | 'invalid' | 'unreadable' | 'unavailable'
  face: {sha256: string; mime: string; bytes: number}
}

type CardMessage = {
  kind: 'nutft/card'
  version: 1
  collection_id: string // e.g. '600B-E1'
  mint: string // HTTPS origin of the mint
  generated_at: number
  asset: CardAsset
  provenance: ProvenanceMessage
}

type RevealMessage = {
  kind: 'nutft/reveal'
  version: 1
  collection_id: string
  mint: string
  source: 'booster' | 'trade' | 'restore'
  assets: CardAsset[] // only after a successful swap at the mint
}

type ProvenanceMessage = {
  kind: 'nutft/provenance'
  version: 1
  collection_id: string
  asset_id: string
  asset_binding: string
  catalog_uri: string
  census_sha256: string
  copies: number
  one_in_packs: number
  state: 'UNSPENT' | 'SPENT' | 'unknown'
  checked_at: number
  bound_to_holder: true // P2BK: bound to you
}

type BalanceMessage = {
  kind: 'bearlett/balance'
  version: 1
  total_sat: number
  by_mint: Array<{
    mint: string
    protocol: 'lnurlcash' | 'cashu'
    sat: number
    pending_sat: number
  }>
  recent: ActivityMessage['entries'] // at most 5
}

type ActivityMessage = {
  kind: 'bearlett/activity'
  version: 1
  entries: Array<{
    id: string
    at: number
    kind: 'receive' | 'pay' | 'mint' | 'transfer' | 'handover'
    sat: number
    status: 'pending' | 'settled' | 'failed'
    mint: string
  }>
}

type OfferMessage = {
  kind: 'bearlett/offer'
  version: 1
  offer: string // BOLT12 offer, not a secret
  amount_sat: number | null
  description: string
  mint: string
  expires_at: number | null
}
```

Kind names follow the existing `lnurlcash/note-design`. A card image is never
part of a message. The surface loads it itself over the Resource NAP and checks
the hash from `face.sha256`.

### 7.5 Example flows

**Open a collection.** Shelf reads `intent.available('collection')` and lists every
installed edition with its title. Opening one sends `collection/open {}` to that
handler. The collection napplet reads its own store, loads images over the Resource
NAP, checks each against `face.sha256`, and renders. With nothing installed, Shelf
says so and links to installation. Never an empty stage.

**Receive a card.** Scanner recognises `cashuB…` with a `nutft` tag, reads the
collection id from the tag, and sends `collection/receive {token}`. The collection
napplet checks the binding against its own catalogue, refuses a token from another
edition, demands unlock, swaps at the mint, stores, and only then sends
`reveal/open RevealMessage`. Reveal shows only what was actually accepted.

**Hand over a card.** The handover screen inside the collection napplet takes the
recipient key by scanner (`scanner/open {expect: 'pubkey'}`) or by input, shows
card, mint and fee, demands unlock, swaps at the mint and shows the result token as
a QR itself. The token never leaves that napplet unless the holder shares it.

**Pay BOLT12.** Scanner or input recognises `lno1…` and sends `pay/open {offer}`.
Pay asks for the amount on an amount-less offer, shows the fee estimate, and sends
`wallet/pay-offer {offer, amountMsat}`. The wallet creates the melt quote, shows amount
plus `fee_reserve`, demands confirmation, melts, and books change. Success is reported
only after booked change, as today.

**Receive BOLT12.** Receive sends `wallet/receive-offer {amount?, description?}`.
The wallet creates the mint quote with its own key and replies `receive/offer
OfferMessage`. Receive shows QR and text. The wallet polls `checkMintQuoteBolt12`
itself and mints the difference of `amount_paid` and `amount_issued`.

## 10. Animation and 3D

### 10.1 Architecture

Every choreography is a HyperFrames composition: clips with `data-start` and
`data-duration`, one seekable timeline per composition. It is executed like the
Nappelin intro: a pure function `state(t)` yields every visible value from
time. A separate renderer paints. No element holds its own animation state. No
`Date.now`. No `Math.random`. Randomness comes from a seeded generator. Reveal,
handover and the detail flip are therefore scrubbable, retimeable, testable in Node, and
renderable as video with `npx hyperframes render`.

### 10.2 Two motion registers

1. **Chrome** follows the Nappelin motion law: 160 to 240 ms ease-out, cuts
   instead of tweens, one overshoot on dock. Nothing floats, bounces or glows.
2. **Cards and scenes** follow HyperFrames and the TCG choreography: depth layers,
   slowly breathing ground, entries 300 to 600 ms with offset, overshoot on
   reveal, glow on charge and burst only on the card.

The Green Law holds in both registers.

### 10.3 Stack decision

| Stage | Technique                                                            | For                                                                       | Cost                                                                                                          |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | CSS 3D: `preserve-3d`, `rotateY`, pointer tilt, `backface-visibility` | Tiles, flip, detail view, handover                                        | 0 KB; proven in the TCG shop                                                                                  |
| 2     | Canvas 2D after the intro pattern                                    | Burst, particles, grain, transitions inside a napplet                     | 0 KB                                                                                                          |
| 3     | Three.js 0.182 with a frame adapter                                  | Booster pack as a body, shelf as a room, foil shader with environment light | `three.module.min.js` 355 KB, 84 KB gzip before tree-shaking; today's wallet napplet is 419 KB, 133 KB gzip |

Recommendation: stages 1 and 2 in every napplet. Stage 3 only in _Reveal_ and _Card_, never
in the Pay family, and only after measuring a built napplet. There is no lazy-load
in a single-file delivery.

### 10.4 Rules

- `prefers-reduced-motion`: cuts instead of tweens, no automatic reveal,
  tilt and breathing off. The end state is identical.
- Tilt and foil only on the focused or touched card. The grid is virtualised.
- Card textures at most 1,024 px wide. Image hash-checked before display.
- Every screen has a visible rest state without script.
- Keyboard: every card focusable, flip and reveal with Enter and Space.

## 11. Technical constraints

From `scripts/napplet-host.mjs:60-61`, [NAPPLETS.md](NAPPLETS.md) and
`node_modules/@napplet/vite-plugin`:

- iframe `sandbox="allow-scripts"`; CSP `default-src 'none'; script-src
'unsafe-inline'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; img-src data: blob:; connect-src 'none';
font-src 'none'`.
- One file per napplet (`artifactMode: 'single-file'`, `assetsInlineLimit` 1 MB);
  no CDN scripts, no external images, no web fonts.
- Manifest kind 35129 with `["archetype", slug, convention]` per role, `requires` per
  NAP domain, unsigned as a build template.
- Network only over the Resource NAP: card images as bytes, checked by SHA-256, shown
  as a blob URL. Blossom mirrors and the 3 MB limit belong in the resource policy.
- One wallet window per storage scope. The shell owns cold-start delivery and
  default handlers. `handler: "<dTag>"` demands user approval.
- Workers over a blob URL are allowed (evidence: avatar-viewer). Cache Storage and
  storage quotas in the napplet are not evidenced.

## 14. Open points and proofs

1. Prove request-reply over two intents in Kehto and Hangar: cold start of
   the core, delivery to the default handler, behaviour with two open surfaces.
2. Write archetype proposals `collection`, `card`, `reveal`, `shelf`, `overview`,
   `pay`, `receive`, `scanner`, `activity` with Boundary and Distinct-from
   and check them against the registry. `wallet` and `bearer-designer` stay local until
   a public implementation exists.
3. Measure a built _Reveal_ napplet with Three.js: bundle, start time, frames per
   second on a mid-range Android device.
4. Check BOLT12 on the mints in use: version, offer with and without amount,
   description, `amount_paid` and `amount_issued` on multiple payments.
5. Resource policy: allow Blossom mirrors, 3 MB limit, behaviour on
   mirror failure and hash mismatch.
6. Storage: card-image cache in the napplet, quotas, behaviour after restart.
7. Fonts: measure the budget per embedded face and decide.
8. Handover: key exchange by QR or intent instead of pasting the P2BK key.
9. Prove `prefers-reduced-motion` and keyboard use in every napplet.
10. Prototype: Shelf, Collection, Card, Reveal and Pay overview in a
    clickable draft with real card data, before napplet code exists.
11. Prove that several napplets of the archetype `collection` can be installed at
    once, that the shell lists them all through `intent.available`, and that the
    default handler does not swallow the others.
12. Fix the per-collection derivation: one BIP39 phrase, the collection id in the
    path, and a restore that scans exactly one keyset. Write test vectors before
    any card is issued against it.
13. Backup with N scopes: one file per collection, or one container that names
    every collection it carries. Decide, then prove a restore of one collection
    while the others stay untouched.
14. Decide whether a true total across collections is worth the
    `handler: "<dTag>"` grant. Shelf works without it.
15. Decide the `wallet` archetype's contract before any Pay surface is built.
    It is closed at four conventions today. `wallet/pay-offer`,
    `wallet/receive-offer`, `wallet/balance` and `wallet/activity` are proposals
    that widen a shipped contract, so they need that decision first, and a
    version marker if they land.
